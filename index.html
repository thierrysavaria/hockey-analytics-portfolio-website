Flagship · Data platform

BU Hockey Analytics Platform

At Niagara, I'd already proven this approach on a lighter stack of Apps Scripts, Sheets and Tableau. At BU, I rebuilt it as a full data platform: ingesting Sportlogiq advanced stats across all of NCAA Division 1, modelling them into coach-facing insight, and delivering it as a lightweight website — no BI licence, no server, works on a phone at the rink.

Design principles

  • Ingest league-wide, deliver team-narrow. Percentiles are meaningless without a full comparison population, so the pipeline pulls all of D1 and filters to BU only at the last step.
  • Never impute missing data. A player who never killed a penalty has structurally absent PK stats, not zeros. The platform treats "no data" and "zero performance" as different facts, everywhere.
  • Compare within a fair cohort. Raw stats reward physical maturity, so the model supports birth-year cohorts alongside full-roster views, and always separates forwards, defencemen and goalies.

What shipped

  • Ingestion layer pulling three distinct Sportlogiq data types (~190-stat comparison set, possession/xG/entries-and-exits, league reference data) with authentication, retry logic and full audit trails.
  • A raw → normalized → delivery database in long format, so adding a new statistic is a one-row insert rather than a schema migration.
  • A configurable weighting model letting coaches define what "good" means by position, with minimum ice-time gates so a player with a handful of shifts can't top a leaderboard by noise.
  • A no-dependency coach-facing site: player cards with areas-of-the-game breakdowns and PDF export, sortable ranking boards, and client-side ice-time sliders that recompute every ranking instantly with zero server.
  • A daily unattended refresh that pulls, rebuilds, regenerates and deploys once the season is live.

Sub-projects served from the same platform

  • Junior-league scouting pilot — a second vendor's API proving out always-current rosters and an automatic "who was just added or traded" detector across 10 junior leagues (USHL, BCHL, AJHL, OJHL and six more).
  • Pre-game report simplification — reduces the vendor's dense 30-page automated scouting PDF to a single readable tale-of-the-tape page a coach will actually read before puck drop.

Research · Tactical

Power play levers and league trends

A deep dive to find high-impact, personnel-independent levers for power play performance — the levers that survive roster turnover, rather than the ones that were really just a departing star.

I integrated three seasons of Sportlogiq event data with iCE metrics and iNSiDE EDGE breakout data, defined elite power play cohorts on the dual criteria of PP% and xG/60, and evaluated 52 variables using an ensemble of Lasso, Ridge and quantile regression. To separate systemic levers from personnel-dependent variance I used an OLS interaction model and Spearman correlation to track metric persistence across roster turnover, with Mann-Whitney tests validating the elite gap. Surviving variables then went through video review for tactical feasibility before anything reached a coach.

52 candidate variables → 4 actionable, turnover-proof levers

Player evaluation · Recruiting

CHL analytical rankings

When CHL players became NCAA-eligible in November, head coach Jason Lammers asked me to rank every 20-year-old in the league. I built a weighted model from deliberately simple, explainable metrics — chosen so I could tell a coach exactly what each one measured and why it mattered — and folded the staff's own scouting notes in alongside the numbers, so they could see where the eye test and the data disagreed.

Optimization 1.0 — automation and reach

I wrote an Apps Script scraper that authenticated into Sportlogiq, pulled the metrics and refreshed the rankings on a schedule. That let me broaden the metric set, generate alternate cuts by position, age group and game situation, and connect the output to a Tableau map highlighting strong players geographically near Niagara for summer visits.

Optimization 2.0 — after conference feedback

I presented this work at the 2025 Sportlogiq Users Annual Conference and rebuilt it on the feedback. Ordinal position ranks became true percentiles on metric values, which exposes the real gaps — a player at 2.0 xG/game sits at the 100th percentile while the next-best at 1.0 falls to the 50th, a separation ordinal ranking hides completely. I then built a second scraper for Strength of Opposition and Strength of Teammates, shipping two complementary versions: one using SOO differential as a simple 1–5 qualitative scale coaches could adjust against mentally, and one folding SOO and SOT directly into the weighted model.

Forecasting

Projected goals model

Eight NCAA seasons of player production, used to derive average year-over-year growth by class — refined with goal-scoring tiers, since a freshman who scored 2 and one who scored 10 don't grow the same way. Individual prediction stayed noisy, as NCAA production swings hard season to season; team-level aggregation held up well.

No forecast → Within 5.5 goals over 37 games

A lot of the real work on this platform wasn't building features — it was catching subtle data-integrity issues that a general software engineer could easily miss but that matter to anyone who knows the sport: stats pooled the wrong way, a metric scored backwards, a percentile pool that didn't respond correctly to a filter. Catching those takes hockey knowledge as much as coding. It's also why the first thing I do with a new model's output is check it against what the staff already believes about their own roster, and treat disagreements as things to investigate, not to explain away.
